Finding cell phone numbers, addresses, photos of documents, or family information in search results can feel alarming. That data can be used to contact you, create a more convincing scam, or create a fake profile. The first step is not to send panic messages to everyone, but rather to understand where the data is and who can delete it.

Google Search displays copies or links from other pages. Deleting results from Google does not always delete the content on the original site. On the other hand, asking the original site to delete content does not always immediately delete views that have been saved in search results. You may need to address both paths.

This article discusses a scalable process: securely search for data, note URLs, submit requests to site owners or Google according to the type of information, then protect accounts and documents from reappearing. Don't share sensitive URLs publicly when asking for help.

## Initial steps before checking

Start with devices and accounts you trust. Don't follow instructions from surprise messages, advertisements, or accounts that ask for secret codes. Log in via the application or site address that you type yourself. Don't use links, help numbers, or forms sent by unverified parties.

Don't treat one result, one symptom, or one social media post as a certainty. Avoid installing apps from random links, sharing verification codes, or giving screen access to unverified parties. Use official service procedures when the problem concerns money, accounts, or phone numbers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can Google delete all my data from the internet?

Not always. Google may assess removal from search results for certain categories, but the source site may keep the content. Contact the source as well whenever possible.

### What data needs to be documented before requesting deletion?

Note the content URL, search result URL, search terms, data type, and screenshots. Don't enter sensitive data that isn't requested by the form.

### Should I pay for data deletion services?

Start with official Google channels and source sites. Be wary of parties who promise instant results or ask for account credentials.

### What if the content contains threats or impersonation?

Save evidence and use the platform's reporting channels. If there are physical threats, extortion, or criminal activity, consider contacting authorities through the appropriate channels.
